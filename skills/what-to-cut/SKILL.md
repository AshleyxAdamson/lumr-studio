# What to cut

The judgment behind every cut that removes words. `tight-cut`, `assemble-takes` and `splice-clips` say how to make a cut with the tools. This says which ones to make.

The creator's words decide. A tool offers a pace, a switch, a list of candidates; none of those is a reason to cut. What the creator asked for is.

## What goes without asking

- Dead air: a wait with nothing in it, the camera being handled, the tail after the sign-off.
- A false start, and the "let me start over" beside it.
- An earlier take of a line the creator recorded again. The last complete take plays, with the question or lead-in that belongs to it.
- An um that starts a sentence or has a clear pause beside it. `tight-cut` step 5 has the test.
- **Padding**: a tail that says its own sentence again and adds nothing. See the test below.

## What stays unless the creator asks

- **Asides.** What the creator said off script is often the best part, and a scripted video needs it most.
- **Jokes**, the line that sets each one up, and the beat after it.
- **A fact said again.** "And it keeps for a week" in the baking part and again in the storing part is two viewers being told once each.
- **A line that says how serious something is**, even when it rambles. "If it over-proofs, I don't know, it just collapses, it's bad" tells the viewer something a clean sentence didn't.
- **A summing-up.** "That's the whole problem in a nutshell" is emphasis. The creator put it there to land the point.
- **A reaction.** "Yeah, that was a letdown" after showing the flat loaf.
- **An um inside the flow of a sentence.**
- **Every "like"**, until the creator asks for likes to go.

Repetition is how people make a point land when they talk. Treat a repeat as content until the test below says otherwise.

## The padding test

Look inside one sentence, its tail and the run-up to it, never across the video. Padding is words that only say that sentence again, or only announce that it is coming.

| The line | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "The rise took most of the day because the kitchen was cold, **so that's what slowed it down. Yeah, it was really the cold.**" | The bold part says the sentence again. It goes; the line ends on "the kitchen was cold". |
| "So how long does it rest? **Well, to get into that, the thing is,** overnight, in the fridge." | The bold part announces an answer and says nothing. It goes; the answer follows the question. A question or lead-in that sets up the line stays: this is the filler between the two. |
| "...which is why I proof it overnight. **And it tastes better, too.**" | New information. It stays. |
| "...and that's why the crust matters. **That's the whole loaf in a nutshell.**" | A summing-up. It stays. |
| "I don't want to think about the timer, **I'd rather not hover over the oven.**" | Two ways of saying one feeling, and the feeling is the point of the video. It stays. |

Expect few. A video that already has its retakes and dead air out has little padding left. If your list is long, you are reading across the video; read it again inside each sentence. A tail you can't tell from a summing-up is a closer call: it stays until the creator picks it.

## "Faster", "tighter", "more momentum"

These ask for shorter pauses, with every sentence left in. Raise the pace. The switches on the page are the creator's, and only they change one: if long pauses are off, the pace has nothing to shorten, so tell them that switch is theirs to turn on.

Say what the pauses gave, as a share of the talk. If it falls short of what they asked for, say so, and say the rest would mean taking out sentences. Then show any padding still in the video, and stop there. Which sentences go is the creator's choice.

## Show a change so it can be judged

A creator can't judge a cut from a time and a reason. Show the words.

For each cut that removes words, two lines: how it plays now, with the words that would go in brackets and a few words either side, and how it would play after. Shorten a long cut inside the brackets to its first and last few words.

> Now: ...because the kitchen was cold, [so that's what slowed it down. Yeah, it was really the cold.] Okay, the shaping...
> After: ...because the kitchen was cold. Okay, the shaping...

Number them, so the creator can answer "1 and 4" or "all but 3". Group the ones you are sure of apart from the closer calls, and say why each of those is one.

What goes without asking is saved in a first draft before you show it, so the join check has read it. Three things are saved only once the creator says which: a closer call, anything from the list of what stays, and any new cut of words in an edit they have already heard.

The creator's ear is the test, and you can't hear. Give them every change to listen to: offer the review page for a first draft, and for a change to an edit they have already heard, make a `preview` at each cut and give its path. Say what you checked (the words, the join check, the picture) and what only their ear can: how each join sounds.

Once the creator has said an export is good, that file stays as it is: `render` writes a new file each time. Give the new file's path beside the approved one's, and say exactly what differs.

## When the creator pushes back

A "no, that one matters" is about the rule, not the one cut.

1. Say back the rule as it now stands, narrower, in one sentence.
2. Apply it to every other cut on your list yourself and withdraw each one that no longer passes, so the creator rejects a kind once.
3. Say plainly if little is left. "There isn't much padding in this video" is a finding.

When the creator says something feels off, such as the pace after another change, measure before you agree or disagree: `get_edit` on each version gives its length, its pace and how much the pace took out. Say what the numbers show, then ask what they watched and where it felt wrong.

# What to cut

The judgment behind every cut that removes words. `tight-cut`, `assemble-takes` and `splice-clips` say how to make a cut with the tools. This says which ones to make.

The creator's words decide. A tool offers a pace, a switch, a list of candidates; none of those is a reason to cut. What the creator asked for is.

## What goes without asking

- Dead air: a wait with nothing in it, the camera being handled, the tail after the sign-off.
- A false start, and the "let me start over" beside it.
- An earlier take of a line the creator recorded again. The last complete take plays, with the question or lead-in that belongs to it.
- **Padding**: a tail that says its own sentence again and adds nothing. See the test below.

## What stays unless the creator asks

- **Asides.** What the creator said off script is often the best part, and a scripted video needs it most.
- **Jokes**, the line that sets each one up, and the beat after it.
- **A fact said again.** "And it keeps for a week" in the baking part and again in the storing part is two viewers being told once each.
- **A line that says how serious something is**, even when it rambles. "If it over-proofs, I don't know, it just collapses, it's bad" tells the viewer something a clean sentence didn't.
- **A summing-up.** "That's the whole problem in a nutshell" is emphasis. The creator put it there to land the point.
- **A reaction.** "Yeah, that was a letdown" after showing the flat loaf.
- **An um inside the flow of a sentence**, and every "like" unless the creator asked for likes to go.

Repetition is how people make a point land when they talk. Treat a repeat as content until the test below says otherwise.

## The padding test

Look inside one sentence and its tail, never across the video. Padding is words that only say that sentence again.

| The line | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "...because the oven was already hot, **was most of the wait. And so, yeah, that was sort of the big thing in terms of timing.**" | The bold part says the sentence again. It goes; the line ends on "already hot". |
| "...or is it somewhere in between? **Well, to answer that question, the thing is,** I stopped weighing the flour." | The bold part is a run-up with nothing in it. It goes. |
| "...which is why I proof it overnight. **And it tastes better, too.**" | New information. It stays. |
| "...and that's why the crust matters. **That's the whole loaf in a nutshell.**" | A summing-up. It stays. |
| "I don't want to think about the timer, **I'd rather not hover over the oven.**" | Two ways of saying one feeling, and the feeling is the point of the video. It stays. |

Expect few. A video that already has its retakes and dead air out has little padding left. If your list is long, you are reading across the video; read it again inside each sentence.

## "Faster", "tighter", "more momentum"

These mean shorter pauses. Raise the pace. The switches on the page are the creator's, and only they change one: if long pauses are off, the pace has nothing to shorten, so tell them that switch is theirs to turn on. A switch the creator didn't ask for stays as it is.

Say what the pauses gave, as a share of the talk. If it falls short of what they asked for, say so, and say the rest would mean taking out sentences. Then offer the padding you found, and stop there. Which sentences go is the creator's choice.

## Show a change so it can be judged

A creator can't judge a cut from a time and a reason. Show the words.

For each cut that removes words, two lines: how it plays now, with the words that would go in brackets and a few words either side, and how it would play after.

> Now: ...because the oven was already hot, [was most of the wait. And so, yeah, that was sort of the big thing in terms of timing.] Okay, the shaping...
> After: ...because the oven was already hot. Okay, the shaping...

Number them, so the creator can answer "1 and 4" or "all but 3". Group the ones you are sure of apart from the ones you are not, and say why each of the second group is a closer call.

Nothing that removes words is saved until the creator says which. Then make a `preview` at each one and give its path: the creator's ear is the test, and you can't hear. Say what you checked (the words, the join check, the picture) and what only they can.

Once the creator has said a version is good, a later change is a new version beside it. Say exactly what differs from the one they approved.

## When the creator pushes back

A "no, that one matters" is about the rule, not the one cut.

1. Say back the rule as it now stands, narrower, in one sentence.
2. Apply it to the rest of your list yourself and withdraw what no longer passes. Don't make the creator reject them one by one.
3. Say plainly if little is left. "There isn't much padding in this video" is a finding.

When the creator says something feels off, such as the pace after another change, measure before you agree or disagree: the lengths, the pace and switches of each version, the pauses left in each. Say what the numbers show, then ask what they watched and where it felt wrong.
